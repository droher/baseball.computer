# Referee Report

**Manuscript:** "Estimating the Unrecorded Game: Hierarchical Bayesian Coverage Models for a Century of Baseball Play-by-Play"
**Venue:** Journal of Quantitative Analysis in Sports
**Reviewer:** Referee 2 (applied Bayesian statistics; missing-data methods)

---

## 1. Summary of the submission

The manuscript treats a century-plus of major-league play-by-play (18.1M events, nominally 1910–2025) as the joint output of two processes: the game and its observation. Outcomes are essentially complete, but *detail* fields — batted-ball trajectory and location, handling fielder, pitch sequence, fielding credit — are missing in ways that depend on scorer, era, and the play's result rather than on the play alone. The authors propose a taxonomy that assigns a missingness mechanism to each *event-dimension* rather than to a variable or a row, and then a family of hierarchical Bayesian "coverage" models (non-centered partial pooling; Bernoulli / reference-class softmax / negative-binomial likelihoods; a shared game-hash split; a paired acceptance gate of convergence diagnostics plus a held-out predictive metric) that estimate both the propensity a detail was recorded and a posterior over the detail itself. Three claims are foregrounded: (i) pre-1988 trajectory recording is MNAR (ground balls appear at roughly half their true share among recorded trajectories), a *learned* propensity correction is "refuted" by a masked backtest for learning the selection effect with the wrong sign while converging cleanly, and the honest deliverable is a MAR point plus a per-class sensitivity ribbon over an unidentified offset δ_c; (ii) shared entity-embedding pretraining multiplies downstream player-effect signal ~6× under cross-fitting/leakage gates; (iii) three designed estimands (contact-label confusion B, fielder responsibility I, shift propensity K) are unidentifiable from a single-source record and are withheld. A publication policy (five tiers, an eight-column provenance contract, posteriors-not-argmax) and a reproducibility/provenance appendix close the paper.

## 2. Recommendation

**Major revision.**

The paper has a genuinely valuable core: the event-dimension missingness ontology, the disciplined refusal to publish point corrections for an unidentified MNAR offset, the "clean fit of the wrong quantity" cautionary result, and the practice of withholding unidentified estimands with written blocking conditions are exactly the intellectual honesty JQAS should reward, and the MNAR framing is more careful than most historical-baseball data work. But as submitted the manuscript does not meet a methods journal's evidentiary bar in four respects that are individually sufficient to require revision: (a) it repeatedly claims *calibrated* posteriors yet contains no calibration evidence whatsoever — not one reliability diagram, no ECE in the body, no held-out HDI coverage — while its acceptance gate scores only discrimination (AUC) and fit (RMSE); (b) its central MNAR conclusions ("refuted," "the offset is right," the ribbon width) rest on a *single* synthetic masked backtest whose masking design the authors chose, with no robustness across designs and no real-data anchor; (c) the sensitivity object it actually ships is marginal (one class at a time) when the authors themselves show the real shift is joint, so the published uncertainty is known to be understated; and (d) the three headline findings are unverifiable from the paper and supplements — only the 11 descriptive published-table queries are provided, none of the fits, backtests, or gates. The related-work section is also too thin for a journal (the selection-model and nonignorable-missingness sensitivity literatures the paper effectively rediscovers are uncited). None of this is fatal — the underlying work appears sound and the fixes are additive — but the paper must supply calibration evidence, broaden the MNAR robustness, separate the statistical contribution from the pipeline description, and make its headline claims checkable before it can be accepted.

## 3. Major comments

**M1. No calibration evidence anywhere, yet "calibrated" is the paper's central selling point.**
The abstract and introduction promise "calibrated probability surfaces where deterministic imputation would fabricate certainty," and the entire thesis is posteriors-over-points. Yet the acceptance gate scores ROC-AUC / PR-AUC (discrimination) and RMSE / total-variation / log-loss (fit) — none of which is a calibration metric — and the manuscript contains not a single reliability diagram, calibration curve, or posterior-predictive check. ECE is named once, in passing, as something added in a Model A "fix wave," but no ECE value or plot appears. For posteriors that are published as the product, the paper must show (i) reliability diagrams for the Bernoulli/softmax surfaces on held-out `TEST` games, and (ii) empirical coverage of the reported 94% HDIs (do the intervals contain held-out truth at nominal rate?) for at least the run-expectancy, park-factor, and transition surfaces. As written, "calibrated" is asserted, not demonstrated. AUC near 0.9 is fully compatible with badly miscalibrated probabilities.

**M2. The MNAR conclusions rest on one self-designed masked backtest; robustness is absent.**
"Refuted," "the form of the correction works," and the ±1.0-nat ribbon width are all established by a single masked backtest in which the authors impose a synthetic scorer-and-era masking pattern, then check recovery. The result is entirely a function of that masking design. Two problems follow. First, the validation of the fixed offset is close to circular: supplying the oracle δ_c *implied by the known masking weights* and recovering the masked share to 0.0001 largely demonstrates that you can invert the mask you injected — it does not establish that the offset parameterization is correct for the *real* selection process, which is unknown. Second, the "refutation" of the learned-γ correction (wrong sign, −0.083) is a property of how survivorship interacts with *this* masking mechanism; a differently shaped mechanism could produce a different sign. The paper needs the backtest repeated across several qualitatively different masking designs (e.g., varying the class-dependence structure, context-dependence, and strength), reporting whether the sign-flip and the ribbon coverage are robust. One simulation cannot carry a claim as strong as "refuted."

**M3. The published sensitivity ribbon is marginal, but the phenomenon is joint — the shipped uncertainty is known to be too narrow.**
The authors correctly note (§5, final paragraph) that a real MNAR shift moves every class on the simplex jointly, while the ribbon sweeps one δ_c at a time, and concede this "can understate joint uncertainty." They then ship the marginal ribbon anyway and defer the joint sweep as an open extension. For a paper whose thesis is honesty about MNAR, publishing a sensitivity object you have already shown to understate the uncertainty is the weakest link in the argument. The joint sweep over the offset *vector* (even a coarse grid, or a low-dimensional structured family such as ground-vs-air) is the honest deliverable and should be in the paper, not deferred — especially since the ribbon is a cheap post-hoc reweight with no refit. Relatedly, the grid width ±{0.25, 0.5, 1.0} nats is "calibrated against the backtest," i.e., tuned so one simulated truth lands inside; that is not a principled bound. Justify the range from something external to the single simulation (a defensible prior on selection strength, or the spread across the M2 designs).

**M4. The paper conflates its engineering pipeline with its statistical contribution.**
Substantial stretches — the deep-learning supplement section, the cross-fitting contract, the publication-tier registry, the eight-column provenance contract, the SQLMesh/DuckLake reproducibility appendix, the "no-MLflow-import" test — are software and data-engineering description, not statistical methodology. The two "negative results" in §6 (the `fold_count=1` in-sample-labeled-as-OOF leak; the linear-probe proxy that pointed the wrong way) are real and instructive, but they are ML-workflow QA findings, not statistical contributions, and should be framed as such. Most consequentially, the "~6× embedding signal" headline is a *within-pipeline permutation-importance* diagnostic with no demonstrated effect on the published posteriors' calibration or accuracy on any estimand — it measures that the embeddings carry more signal, not that any published surface is better or better-calibrated as a result. Either connect the 6× to a downstream improvement on the estimand (and its calibration), or demote it from a headline contribution to a pipeline note. A referee needs to see, distilled, what is the *transferable statistical method* versus the description of one system's build.

**M5. The identification claims for B, I, K are argued at very unequal rigor, and K is not an identification result at all.**
Only Model B is given a genuine empirical identification argument (the 702/6.0M ≈ 0.0117% recorded-vs-deduced disagreement, plus the absence of an independent second label). Even there, the logic is imprecise: a near-identity confusion matrix Ω means confusion is *rare*, which is a different statement from Ω being *unidentifiable*; the load-bearing argument is the lack of an independent second label (which the deduced class is not), and the paper should lead with that and drop the "collapses onto the prior" gloss, which conflates "small effect" with "non-identified." Model I's argument is a *data-availability* claim (no positioning prior exists in the source), not an identification proof — reasonable, but different in kind. Model K "was not built in this pass" — that is unfinished scope, not an identification failure, and presenting it under "What the record cannot tell you" alongside B overstates the identification narrative. Separate "unidentifiable from this record" (B, I) from "not yet built" (K).

**M6. The headline findings are unverifiable from the paper and supplements.**
The supplement provides only the 11 descriptive queries/outputs against the *published* tables (which I checked and which are internally consistent with the prose). None of the evidence for the three foregrounded contributions is included or reconstructable: the masked backtest (M2), the γ-refutation numbers, the oracle-δ recovery, the 6.4×/6.3× permutation-importance gate, the ablation "moves posteriors < 0.25 SD," the leak-vs-refit 0.5031/0.4916 comparison, and every R-hat/ESS/divergence figure are stated but not shown. The Reproducibility section describes infrastructure (artifact manifests, sampler configs) that a referee cannot access. So the paper's *descriptive* claims are checkable and its *inferential* claims are not — the reverse of what matters. At minimum, provide as supplement the backtest harness and its outputs, the gate reports, and the ablation/diagnostic tables for the models whose findings the paper rests on.

**M7. Every published surface is `exploratory`; none has cleared the paper's own gate.**
The results section presents 337M rows as findings, yet the inventory shows every populated table stamped `confidence_status = exploratory`, and the text concedes "none has yet cleared the stricter `passed` gate." The paper simultaneously argues the paired gate *is* the discipline and ships results that have not passed it. This must be reconciled: state precisely what fails the `passed` gate (is it M1 calibration? held-out coverage? something else?), and either present the surfaces as provisional throughout or clear the gate. As written, the central deliverable undercuts the central methodological claim.

**M8. Related work is inadequate for a journal and omits the literatures the paper rediscovers.**
The selection-model half of the treatment (§5 writes an explicit selection process R ~ Bernoulli(s(c,x)) acting on the latent class) is the Heckman (1979) selection-model tradition, uncited. The sensitivity ribbon over an unidentified offset is precisely the *delta-adjusted / tipping-point pattern-mixture sensitivity analysis* standard in the nonignorable-missingness literature (Scharfstein–Rotnitzky–Robins 1999; Molenberghs & Kenward 2007; the clinical-trials "tipping point" analyses) — the paper reinvents it and cites only Little (1993). The informative-nonresponse literature in official statistics / survey methodology is directly on point and absent. On the sports side, two citations (openWAR; Marchi & Albert) do not constitute a related-work section for a data-quality/measurement paper; the Retrosheet data-quality and measurement-error-in-tracking-data literatures are missing. The paper should position its contribution *against* these, particularly to make clear what (if anything) is methodologically new beyond applying delta-adjustment sensitivity analysis to a new and unusually vivid dataset.

**M9. HDI coverage under sparsity is asserted, not validated.**
The park-factor argument ("wider HDI for NAL is the model correctly reporting what it does not know") is presented as self-evidently correct behavior. Wider intervals under less data are *necessary* but not *sufficient* for correctness — the intervals could be uniformly over- or under-confident. This is the same gap as M1: without held-out coverage checks, "honest uncertainty" is a narrative about interval width, not a demonstrated calibration property. The `linear_weights_estimated` limitation the authors already disclose (RE-posterior-only bands that ignore finite-sample cell uncertainty, so a sparse cell reports an interval as tight as a dense one) is a concrete instance of intervals that are *not* honest under sparsity — it directly contradicts the park-factor narrative and should be reconciled rather than left in the limitations list.

## 4. Minor comments

1. **Model counting is inconsistent.** The abstract says "twelve probabilistic surfaces … ten populated, two deferred"; §6/policy say "three model letters out of eleven attempted"; letters run A–K. Provide one table mapping letter → estimand → published table → tier → status, and use it consistently.
2. **Span inconsistency.** The abstract/intro say 1910–2025 and 18.1M events, but `coverage_by_decade` has a "1900" row, park factors include 1940s Negro-league and 1941–43 seasons, and Model G "pools 1901 through 2025." State the actual event universe and the actual season span, and reconcile the pre-1910 rows.
3. **Garbled sentence.** "Model D is Model D's first publication" (§ Models E and D) — repair.
4. **Units.** δ_c is described as "log-odds" in §5 and "nats" for the grid; a logit is in nats only if natural-log based — state the base once and be consistent.
5. **94% HDI** is an unusual default (cf. 89% McElreath / 95% conventional); one sentence on why 94 would help, since every interval in the results uses it.
6. **Illustrative tables.** Several results tables show only extremes (2 of 24 RE states; top/bottom park-seasons). Label these as illustrative and, where a claim depends on the full surface (e.g., linear-weights agreement across "all 20 play types"), put the full table in the supplement.
7. **Define s(c,x).** The selection equation would read more cleanly with the range of s and an explicit statement of why the class-independent part cancels under softmax (the same invariance used to kill the scalar RE — cross-reference it).
8. **Tone.** Editorializing asides ("exactly where a fan would expect it," "a lesson worth its own telling," "the offset is right, and the offset is unidentified") should be trimmed for journal register.
9. **Section length.** "Published surfaces" reads as a data tour and is the longest section; compress to the tables that carry an argument, move the rest to supplement.
10. **Provenance comments.** The `<!-- src: … -->` scaffolding must be stripped before submission (the authors note this); several point to internal repo paths that a reader cannot resolve and that currently function as uncheckable citations.

## 5. Questions to the authors

1. Where is the calibration evidence? Please add reliability diagrams and held-out HDI coverage; AUC/RMSE do not speak to calibration, which is the paper's claim.
2. Does the "refuted / validated" MNAR result survive alternative masking-generation designs, or is the sign-flip specific to the one synthetic pattern you imposed?
3. Can you provide the *joint* offset-vector sweep (even coarse) rather than the marginal ribbon, given you show the real shift is joint and the reweight is refit-free?
4. On what basis other than one backtest is the ±1.0-nat grid width chosen? What is the principled upper bound on selection strength?
5. Is Model B's claim "Ω ≈ identity (confusion is rare)" or "Ω is non-identified (we cannot tell)"? The disagreement-rate evidence supports the former; the paper argues the latter.
6. Model K reads as unbuilt scope rather than an identification failure — why is it grouped with B and I under "what the record cannot tell you"?
7. Given every table is `exploratory`, what precisely fails the `passed` gate, and should the results be framed as provisional throughout?
8. Will you release the backtest harness, gate reports, and ablation/diagnostic tables so the three headline findings are verifiable, as the descriptive tables already are?
9. Does the 6× permutation-importance gain translate into a measured improvement (accuracy *and* calibration) on any published estimand, or is it only an internal embedding diagnostic?
10. How do you reconcile the park-factor "honest uncertainty" narrative with the disclosed `linear_weights_estimated` bands that ignore finite-sample cell uncertainty and are therefore too tight on sparse cells?
